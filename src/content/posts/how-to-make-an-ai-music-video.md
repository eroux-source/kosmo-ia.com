To make an AI music video, you write a treatment that maps the song section by section, design a visual world and a locked set of character references, generate short shots with image and video models, edit them to the beat, upscale and grade the final cut, then clear the rights and apply the platform's AI disclosure before release. The song drives every decision: its structure sets the number of shots, its tempo sets the cut points, and its mood sets the look. A four-minute video typically needs dozens of finished shots, and many more generations than that, because video models still produce artifacts and drift. The process in seven steps:

1. Write a treatment and a shot list mapped to the song's timeline.
2. Build the visual world: style frames, palette, lenses, locations.
3. Lock the characters (and the artist's likeness, with written consent).
4. Generate stills, then animate them into short clips.
5. Edit to the beat, with cuts on downbeats and section changes.
6. Upscale, grade, and fix artifacts in post.
7. Clear the song license and likeness rights, then label the video on each platform.

## Step 1: Write the treatment

A treatment is a short document describing the idea, story arc and look. For an AI music video, add a timeline: list each section of the song (intro, verse, chorus, bridge, outro) with its timecode and assign a visual beat to each. This becomes your shot list.

Keep the concept achievable. AI video is strongest with dreamlike transitions, surreal worlds, stylized animation and continuous camera moves. It is weakest with lip-sync over long takes, complex choreography and hands playing instruments. Paul Trillo's video for Washed Out's "The Hardest Part" is a good model: according to Tom's Guide, it is a single continuous fly-through following a couple across years of their life, assembled from 55 Sora clips, an idea that suits how generation models move.

## Step 2: Build the visual world

Before generating any video, lock the look in stills: a style board of 10 to 20 frames from an image model (Midjourney, Flux or similar) that fixes palette, lighting, lens and grain. Reuse one style prompt in every generation and save the references that work.

### Choose a format early

Decide the master aspect ratio before you generate: 16:9 for YouTube, 9:16 for Shorts, Reels and TikTok. If you also need a Spotify Canvas, Spotify's guidelines specify a vertical 9:16 loop of 3 to 8 seconds, 720 to 1080 pixels tall, with no talking or singing footage because it will not sync with the song.

## Step 3: Keep characters consistent

Character drift is the problem audiences notice first. Create a character sheet for each recurring person (front, three-quarter, profile, full body, wardrobe) and use it as the reference in every shot. Most current video tools accept image references for this. Limit the number of recurring characters, and design wardrobe that is easy to reproduce. Even in the Washed Out video, Tom's Guide noted that consistency was strong but not perfect.

### The artist's likeness

If the video features the artist, get written consent covering their face and voice, how the likeness may be used, and for how long. In the US, Tennessee's ELVIS Act, in effect since July 1, 2024, added voice (including AI-simulated voice) to the protected rights of name, image and likeness, according to the American Bar Association. Never use the face or voice of another real artist without permission.

## Step 4: Generate the shots

Work from stills to motion. Generate or refine the key frame for each shot, then animate it with an image-to-video model, which gives you far more control than text-to-video. Generate several takes per shot and keep a naming system that ties each clip to its timecode.

### AI music video tools compared

| Tool | Main use in a music video | Strength | Limit |
|---|---|---|---|
| Midjourney | Style frames, key frames | Strong art direction | Stills only for most workflows |
| Flux | Key frames, character stills | Fine control, custom models | Needs setup for consistency |
| Runway | Image-to-video, editing tools | Camera control, references | Short clip lengths |
| Kling | Image-to-video | Realistic motion | Can drift on faces in long shots |
| Veo | Text and image-to-video | High realism | Access and cost vary by plan |
| Sora | Text and image-to-video | Long, continuous moves | Artifacts in complex physics |
| Higgsfield | Camera moves, presets | Fast stylized motion | Less control over fine detail |
| Topaz Video AI | Upscaling, frame interpolation | Sharper masters | Can over-sharpen faces |

No single tool does everything. The Weeknd's 2024 teaser for his São Paulo concert, directed by Yza Voku, combined Midjourney stills with animations from Runway Gen-3, Luma Dream Machine and Google's Veo, as VentureBeat reported. Mixing tools per shot is normal.

## Step 5: Edit to the beat

The edit is where an AI music video becomes a music video. Import the song first, mark the beats and section changes, and cut picture to sound, not the other way around.

- Place hard cuts on downbeats and major transitions on section changes (verse to chorus).
- Use slower, longer shots in verses and faster cutting in choruses.
- Hide generation artifacts with cut timing: most clips have a clean first second and degrade later.
- Use speed ramps and match cuts to join clips from different generations.

A standard editor is enough: Tom's Guide reports Trillo assembled his clips in Premiere with light After Effects work.

## Step 6: Upscale, grade and finish

Generated clips often have soft detail. Upscale the locked edit, not every take, then grade the whole video so clips from different models match. Finish with a quality pass for flicker, warped hands and melting backgrounds; compositing fixes what regeneration cannot.

## Step 7: Clear the rights and follow platform rules

### Song licensing

The label and publisher must approve use of the master recording and composition in the video. If the artist commissions the video, this is usually covered, but confirm it in writing. If you generate any music with AI tools such as Suno, check the tool's commercial terms. The industry is moving to licensed models: according to Euronews, Universal Music Group settled its lawsuit with Udio in 2025 and announced a licensed creation platform for 2026.

### Copyright in the video itself

The US Copyright Office concluded in January 2025 that prompts alone do not make you the author of an AI output, while human selection, arrangement and modification can be protected, as Jones Day summarizes. Keep records of your treatment, edit and post work.

### YouTube and Spotify

YouTube requires creators to disclose realistic altered or synthetic content, and lists AI-generated music as a case that needs disclosure, according to its Help Center. Clearly fantastical content does not, but a realistic artist performance generated by AI does. Spotify Canvas content must follow Spotify's Canvas guidelines and content policy. Brands releasing in the EU should also note that the AI Act's transparency rules have applied since August 2026.

## FAQ

### How long does it take to make an AI music video?

A short visualizer can take days, while a full narrative video with consistent characters takes weeks. At KOSMO® AI, projects run 3 to 4 weeks from an approved brief. Most of the time goes into references, selecting takes, editing and post, not generation.

### What is the best AI music video generator?

There is no single best tool. Most productions use an image model such as Midjourney or Flux for key frames, video models such as Runway, Kling, Veo or Sora for motion, and a standard editor for the cut.

### Can I put the artist in an AI music video?

Yes, with written consent from the artist covering their face and voice and the scope of use. Laws such as Tennessee's ELVIS Act protect voice and likeness against unauthorized AI use. Never generate another real artist without permission.

### Do I have to label an AI music video on YouTube?

Yes, when it contains realistic AI-generated or altered content, including AI-generated music, according to YouTube's Help Center. Obviously fantastical or animated content does not require the disclosure. Persistent failure to disclose can lead to labels being applied, content removal or Partner Program suspension.

### Can I use an AI music video as a Spotify Canvas?

You can cut a Canvas from it if it follows Spotify's specs: a vertical 9:16 loop of 3 to 8 seconds, without singing footage or rapid cuts.
