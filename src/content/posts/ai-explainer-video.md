An AI explainer video is a short animated or live-action-style film that explains a product, service or idea, produced with generative AI for visuals, motion or voice and finished by editors and animators. It follows the same rules as any explainer: one problem, one solution, one call to action, usually in 60 to 90 seconds for a homepage and 15 to 30 seconds for ads. AI changes the production, not the craft. It speeds up concept frames, style exploration, scene generation, voiceover drafts and versioning, which used to take most of the timeline. It does not write a clear message for you, keep a character or interface consistent on its own, or guarantee that your product looks right. A good AI explainer video comes from:

1. A script built around one buyer and one problem, tested before production.
2. A length matched to where the video will play.
3. A style chosen for what you need to show (UI, a physical product, an abstract idea).
4. A voiceover with clear commercial rights.
5. A finishing pass by people: edit, product accuracy, sound and captions.

## Why explainer video production still matters

Explainers are one of the most common business videos. According to Wyzowl's Video Marketing Statistics 2026 survey, 68% of video marketers have made explainer videos, second only to social media videos, and 96% of consumers say they have watched an explainer to learn about a product or service. The same survey found 63% of people would most like to learn about a product by watching a short video.

AI is now part of the workflow for most teams. Wyzowl reports that 63% of video marketers have used AI video tools to create or edit marketing videos, up from 51% a year earlier.

## How to structure an AI explainer video script

The script is where most explainers succeed or fail. Write it first, read it aloud, and only then generate anything.

### The four-part structure

1. **Problem (about 15% of runtime).** Name the pain in the buyer's words. "Your team spends Monday morning rebuilding the same report."
2. **Solution (about 15%).** Introduce the product as the answer, in one sentence.
3. **How it works (about 50%).** Three steps or three features, never more. Each one gets a visual.
4. **Proof and call to action (about 20%).** One credible proof point, then a single next step ("Start a free trial", "Book a demo").

### Count words, not ideas

Bread & Beyond, an explainer studio, says its voice actors read at about 150 words per minute and recommends roughly 140 to 150 words for a 60 second script, fewer for a slower, more emotional read. Timing it this way stops the most common problem: a 90 second script crammed into 60 seconds of animation.

### Write for the screen

- One idea per sentence, one sentence per scene.
- Let visuals carry what words don't need to say: if the screen shows a dashboard, the voice doesn't describe it.
- Write the on-screen text separately and keep it short; many people watch muted.
- Read the script to someone in sales. If they can't repeat the value proposition, rewrite.

## How long should an explainer video be?

Length depends on where the video plays and how warm the viewer is. Wistia's 2025 analysis of video length found that videos under one minute averaged a 52% engagement rate, with product videos at 50%, and that educational and tutorial videos of one to five minutes still held engagement above 50%.

| Use case | Typical length | Approx. script words (at 150 wpm) | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Paid social ad | 15 to 30 s | 35 to 75 | Benefit in the first seconds, 9:16 |
| Homepage or landing page explainer | 60 to 90 s | 140 to 225 | The main product explainer video |
| Feature or release video | 30 to 60 s | 75 to 150 | One feature, one outcome |
| Sales deck or outbound video | 45 to 90 s | 110 to 225 | Tailored to one segment |
| Onboarding or tutorial | 1 to 3 min | 150 to 450 | Screen-led, step by step |
| Product walkthrough or demo | 2 to 5 min | 300 to 750 | For warm, evaluating buyers |

For a SaaS explainer video, plan the 60 to 90 second master first, then cut 15 and 30 second versions from it rather than writing separate films.

## Choosing a style: 2D, 3D or live-action-like

The style should follow what you have to explain. AI makes all three faster, but each has its own failure points.

### 2D animation and motion graphics

Best for software, services and abstract ideas. Flat illustration, icons and animated UI keep attention on the message. AI helps generate illustration styles and backgrounds; motion designers still animate the interface so text and buttons stay legible.

### 3D animation

Best for physical products, hardware, devices and anything with a mechanism. A 3D model of the product guarantees accuracy from every angle, and AI can generate the environments around it. This is the safest route for a product explainer video, because generative models tend to invent details on real products.

### Live-action-like (photoreal AI)

Best for lifestyle context: people using the product, a store, a city. Video models such as Veo, Kling, Runway and Sora can produce photoreal scenes, but faces, hands and on-screen text still need checking frame by frame, and recurring characters drift without a fixed reference. Avoid real people's likenesses unless you have consent.

| Style | Best for | AI speeds up | Watch out for |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2D / motion graphics | SaaS, services, concepts | Style frames, illustrations, backgrounds | Unreadable generated text in UI |
| 3D | Physical products, devices | Environments, lighting, variations | Product needs a real 3D model |
| Live-action-like | Lifestyle, people, places | Scenes that would need a shoot | Face and character drift, uncanny hands |
| Hybrid (UI + 3D + footage) | Most B2B explainers | Every stage | Keeping one visual system |

Animated explainers sit close to animated commercials; if your goal is brand awareness rather than education, look at [AI animated commercial production](/ai-animated-commercials).

## Where AI speeds up explainer production

- **Concept and style frames:** two or three looks in days instead of weeks, with Midjourney or Flux for stills.
- **Storyboards and animatics:** every scene roughed out before animation starts, so feedback happens early.
- **Scene generation and extension:** backgrounds, transitions and b-roll generated, then composited.
- **Voiceover drafts:** a synthetic temp voice to time the animatic, replaced or approved later.
- **Versioning:** new languages, markets, screen updates and cutdowns without restarting the project.

AI is weaker at the parts buyers notice most: exact UI, brand colors, logos and product proportions. Those are rebuilt or composited by designers.

## AI voiceover: ElevenLabs and the rights question

AI voice tools such as ElevenLabs produce natural narration in many languages, which makes localized explainers far cheaper. Check the license before you publish. According to ElevenLabs' help center, its free plan carries no commercial license and requires attribution, while paid plans include a commercial license (beta features excluded). ElevenLabs also states that you can only create a Professional Voice Clone of your own voice, even with someone else's consent; that person must create and verify the clone and share it with you.

Practical rules:

- Generate final audio on a paid plan, and keep a record of which account and plan produced it.
- Never clone a founder's or actor's voice without their written consent and the tool's verification.
- Direct the read: pace, emphasis and pronunciation of product names matter more than voice choice.
- For flagship films, a human voice actor is still worth comparing against the AI take.

## Common explainer video mistakes

1. **Too many features.** Three is the ceiling. The rest goes in a tutorial.
2. **Starting with the company.** Start with the viewer's problem.
3. **Generating before the script is locked.** Every script change after generation costs shots.
4. **Letting AI draw the product or UI.** Use real screens and a real 3D model.
5. **No captions.** Many viewers watch muted; captions are part of the edit.
6. **One length for every channel.** Cut versions for ads, site and sales.
7. **Inconsistent characters.** Lock a reference set before production.
8. **Unclear rights.** Confirm commercial use for every tool, voice and music track.

## FAQ

### How much does an AI explainer video cost?

It depends on length, style and how much custom animation or 3D the product needs. AI reduces the time spent on style frames, scenes and versions, which usually lowers cost compared with fully hand-animated production. Ask for a fixed quote based on a script and a style reference.

### How long should a SaaS explainer video be?

Plan 60 to 90 seconds for the main homepage film, roughly 140 to 225 words of voiceover. Cut 15 to 30 second versions for paid social and 30 to 60 second clips for individual features.

### Can AI make the whole explainer video?

AI can generate most of the frames, motion and a draft voiceover, but not a finished explainer that represents your product accurately. People still write the script, rebuild UI and product shots, fix consistency and finish sound and captions.

### Is AI voiceover good enough for an explainer?

For most explainers, yes, especially for localized versions. Use a paid plan with a commercial license, direct the pacing and pronunciation, and compare against a human read for flagship films.

### Which style is best for a product explainer video?

For a physical product, 3D, because a real model keeps the product accurate from every angle. For software, 2D motion graphics with redesigned UI. Live-action-like AI works for lifestyle context but needs careful checks on faces and hands.
