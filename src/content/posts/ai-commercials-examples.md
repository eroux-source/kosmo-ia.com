AI commercials are ads whose footage is mostly or entirely produced with generative video models (such as Veo, Sora, Kling or Runway) instead of a live-action shoot, then finished by human editors, artists and sound teams. Since 2024, major brands have aired them on TV, streaming and social, with very mixed results. The clearest AI commercial examples so far, and the lesson each one teaches:

1. **Coca-Cola, "Holidays Are Coming" (2024, 2025):** online backlash, top effectiveness scores. Brand codes beat novelty.
2. **Toys"R"Us, Sora brand film (2024):** dreamlike stories hide AI's realism flaws.
3. **Kalshi, NBA Finals (2025):** made in two days with Veo 3. Absurd and fast can beat polish.
4. **Svedka, Super Bowl LX (2026):** AI scales best with a character you own.
5. **Under Armour (2024):** criticized for uncredited footage. Rights still apply.
6. **McDonald's Netherlands (2025):** pulled within days. Tone fails before technology does.

## The best AI commercials by real brands, one by one

### Coca-Cola, "Holidays Are Coming" (2024 and 2025)

Coca-Cola's first AI-generated version of its holiday trucks ad, in 2024, was largely poorly received online, NBC reported. In 2025 it returned with two spots from the AI studios Silverside and Secret Level.

The Wall Street Journal reported that Silverside prompted, generated and refined more than 70,000 video clips for its film, with five AI specialists on that part of the work, and about 100 people across the whole campaign. CMO Manolo Arroyo told the WSJ that traditional holiday productions started about a year ahead, while AI let them finish in around a month.

Online, viewers again called the films "soulless," according to NBC. Yet System1, which tests ads with real viewers for emotional response, gave the 2025 film its maximum score of 5.9 stars and concluded that AI did not meaningfully change how much people enjoyed it.

**What it teaches:** comment sections and effectiveness tests can disagree. The trucks, Santa and the jingle did the work; AI reproduced brand equity, it did not invent it.

### Toys"R"Us, "The Origin of Toys"R"Us" (2024)

In June 2024, Toys"R"Us and the agency Native Foreign released a roughly one-minute brand film made with Sora, after a private premiere during Cannes Lions. It imagines founder Charles Lazarus as a boy, then drifts into a toy-filled dream. Digiday reported the team generated hundreds of shots and narrowed them to a few dozen.

Some viewers found it creepy and noticed young Lazarus's face changing between shots; others praised the storytelling. One PR executive quoted by Digiday noted that the dream framing hid Sora's weakness with realistic visuals.

**What it teaches:** pick a story that fits what the model does well. Dreams, memories and stylized worlds forgive small inconsistencies; a realistic close-up of a recurring human face does not.

### Kalshi, NBA Finals spot (2025)

The prediction market Kalshi asked director P.J. Accetturo for the most unhinged NBA Finals commercial possible. According to NPR, he used Gemini and ChatGPT to shape the script and convert it into shot-by-shot prompts, generated the footage with Google's Veo 3, and needed 300 to 400 generations to get 15 usable clips. The whole job took two days, and Kalshi said the cost of prompting was under $2,000 (Accetturo's own fee was not disclosed).

The 30-second ad debuted in the YouTube TV stream of Game 3 of the NBA Finals in June 2025 and passed 3 million views on Kalshi's X account within a week, NPR reported. One analyst quoted there needed several viewings to understand what was being advertised.

**What it teaches:** AI shines when the brief embraces its strangeness, but a fast cut of wild scenes still needs a clear product moment.

### Svedka, "Shake Your Bots Off" (Super Bowl LX, 2026)

For its first Super Bowl ad, Svedka worked with Silverside on a 30-second spot that its parent company described as primarily AI-generated. TechCrunch, citing the WSJ, reported that it took about four months to rebuild Svedka's long-running Fembot character and train the AI to reproduce her facial expressions and body movements. Humans still wrote the storyline.

The spot was polarizing, but it got people talking, which is much of a Super Bowl brief.

**What it teaches:** the costly part of a quality AI commercial is often setup, not generation. A consistent character takes time, then pays off across future ads.

### Under Armour with Anthony Joshua (2024)

In March 2024, director Wes Walker shared an Under Armour spot featuring boxer Anthony Joshua that he called the first AI-powered sports commercial. He said the team had no athlete access, only existing assets including a 3D model, and three weeks to deliver a mix of AI video, 3D CGI, VFX and 35mm footage.

TechCrunch reported that creatives quickly pointed out that some athlete footage came from an earlier commercial by director Gustav Johansson and was not credited at first. Credits were added after a public argument.

**What it teaches:** "AI-powered" does not clean the rights on source material. Any footage, image or likeness that feeds a model or an edit needs clear licensing and, where relevant, credit.

### McDonald's Netherlands, "The most terrible time of the year" (2025)

In December 2025, McDonald's Netherlands released a 45-second AI-generated ad, made by the agency TBWA\Neboko and the production company The Sweetshop, showing holiday mishaps set to a reworked Christmas classic. NBC News reported that after a wave of criticism calling it creepy and cynical, McDonald's removed it within days. The production company's CEO said ten people had worked full-time for five weeks, more hours than a traditional shoot.

**What it teaches:** AI is not cheap or quick by default, and it cannot rescue a concept that feels off for the season.

## What successful AI generated commercials have in common

### They start from a strong idea, not from the tool

Kalshi's brief was a tone ("unhinged"), Coca-Cola's a decades-old brand asset. In both, AI served an idea that would have worked with a camera. Ads that lead with "made with AI" attract the AI debate instead of attention for the product.

### They avoid the uncanny valley, or make it part of the style

Realistic human faces in close-up remain the riskiest shot. Animals, mascots, stylized worlds, products and landscapes are safer. Toys"R"Us chose a dream, Coca-Cola leaned on animals in 2025, and Svedka used a robot.

### They are finished by people, on a realistic schedule

Every serious example added human editing, compositing, color and sound. Kalshi shows the floor (two days); Svedka and McDonald's show that consistency and polish can take weeks or months. For budgets, see our guide on [how much an AI commercial costs](/blog/ai-commercial-cost).

## The backlash is real: how to plan for it

Most of these AI commercial examples drew some negative reaction. NPR cited a 2024 IAB study finding that only 48% of Gen Z and millennial consumers felt positively about AI-generated ads. Before you air one, check these points:

- **Disclosure:** decide upfront how you will label AI use. YouTube and Meta have rules for realistic synthetic content, and FTC rules on deceptive advertising still apply.
- **Likeness and consent:** never generate a real person without a signed agreement.
- **Source rights:** know where every reference, clip, voice and music track comes from.
- **Consistency:** check products, logos and characters frame by frame.
- **Category fit:** a warm holiday ad is judged more harshly than a deliberately weird comedy spot.

## FAQ

### What was the first AI commercial by a major brand?

Toys"R"Us and Native Foreign presented what was billed as the first brand film made with OpenAI's Sora in June 2024. Earlier campaigns used AI for parts of an ad; this was one of the first widely covered spots built mainly from text-to-video.

### Did Coca-Cola's AI commercial work?

It depends on how you measure. The 2024 and 2025 films drew heavy criticism on social media, but System1's viewer testing gave the 2025 film its top score of 5.9 stars, and Coca-Cola said the 2024 film performed well. The lesson most analysts drew is that the familiar brand assets drove the result.

### Are AI commercials allowed on TV?

Yes. Kalshi's spot ran in the NBA Finals stream on YouTube TV and Svedka's aired during the Super Bowl. The usual standards apply: cleared rights, accurate claims and any disclosure your platforms require.

### Why do some AI commercials get backlash?

The most common triggers are uncanny human faces, a sense that the brand is cutting artists out, and a tone that clashes with the moment, as in McDonald's Netherlands' Christmas ad. Uncredited reuse of other people's work, as in the Under Armour case, also draws fast criticism.

### What makes an AI commercial look professional?

A clear concept, shots chosen to suit the model, and serious post-production. The best examples add human editing, compositing, color, sound and licensed music, and check product accuracy frame by frame.
