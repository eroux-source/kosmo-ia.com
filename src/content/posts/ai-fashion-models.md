AI fashion models are computer-generated people used to wear and present clothing in campaigns, lookbooks and e-commerce images. They come in three forms: a stock AI model from an image tool, a custom synthetic character designed for one brand, or a digital twin, which is a licensed AI replica of a real model. The clothes are usually real products; the person, pose and setting are generated. Brands use AI generated models to cover more sizes, markets and colorways faster, but every major launch has raised the same questions:

1. **Consent:** if the model is based on a real person, did that person agree in writing, for what use, for how long and for how much?
2. **Jobs:** does the AI model replace models, photographers, stylists, hair and makeup artists?
3. **Diversity:** is a generated person of color, or a generated plus-size body, a substitute for hiring real ones?
4. **Disclosure:** does the audience know the person is not real, and does the law require a label?
5. **Accuracy:** does the garment on the AI model look like the one the customer receives?

## What AI fashion models are

An AI fashion model is not an avatar in a game or a 3D mannequin. It is a photorealistic person produced by image and video models (tools such as Midjourney, Flux, Higgsfield, Veo or Kling, depending on the job), then refined in retouch. Digital models in fashion existed before generative AI, as CGI characters built in 3D software, but generative tools made them far cheaper and faster to produce, which is why brands moved from experiments to catalogs.

### Stock, custom and digital twin

The distinction matters legally. A stock or custom AI model does not depict any identifiable person, so the main issues are disclosure and audience trust. A digital twin depicts a real person, so consent, pay and duration of use come first.

## Real brand cases of AI generated models

### Levi's and Lalaland.ai (2023)

In March 2023, Levi Strauss announced a partnership with Lalaland.ai to test AI-generated models, presented as a way to show products on more body types and skin tones. The backlash was immediate, as PetaPixel reported: critics argued that the budget could hire diverse human models instead, and *Spider-Man: Into the Spider-Verse* director Peter Ramsey called the move "laziness, cheapness, and cynicism." Levi's told PetaPixel the technology would supplement, not replace, its photo shoots and that it would keep diversifying its human models, and it later added an apology to its press release.

### Mango Teen (2024)

In July 2024, Mango presented a campaign for its Mango Teen line that FashionUnited described as the brand's first AI-generated campaign. Mango said it was built by its own teams (design, styling, dataset and AI model training, photography studio) and that it had developed more than fifteen AI platforms since 2018. Its chief information technology officer framed AI as a "co-pilot" for employees rather than a replacement.

### H&M digital twins (2025)

In March 2025, H&M said it would create AI digital twins of 30 models, including Mathilda Gvarliani, according to FashionUnited. The models keep control over how their replicas are used and are paid for them as they are for photographs, and H&M said the first images would carry watermarks. Model Alliance founder Sara Ziff still raised concerns about consent, compensation and the knock-on effect on hair and makeup jobs, as reported by ABC News from a CNN interview.

### Guess in Vogue (2025)

The August 2025 issue of American Vogue carried a Guess ad featuring AI-generated models created by the London agency Seraphinne Vallora. PetaPixel reported that the only disclosure was a small credit line, "Produced by Seraphinne Vallora on AI," and that Vogue said the ad was not an editorial decision. Readers criticized both the brand and the magazine on social media, mainly over beauty standards and lost work for models and photographers.

## Likeness consent and model rights

### The New York Fashion Workers Act

New York's Fashion Workers Act took effect on June 19, 2025, according to Morgan Lewis. It defines a digital replica as a significant computer-generated or AI-enhanced version of a model's face, body or voice. Clients (retailers, designers, ad agencies, photographers, publishers) must obtain clear, conspicuous and prior written consent before creating or using a model's digital replica, detailing scope, purpose, rate of pay and duration. Model management companies need that consent separately from the representation agreement and face civil penalties of up to $3,000 for a first violation and $5,000 for later ones.

### What a digital twin contract should cover

Whatever the state, a solid agreement covers the uses allowed (channels, markets, product categories), the term, the pay per use or per period, the right to approve outputs, what happens to the training data at the end, and exclusivity. Without it, you are exposed under right of publicity laws, not only in New York.

## The diversity debate

The Levi's case set the terms of the debate. Supporters argue that AI generated models let small brands show a garment on many body types and skin tones they could never afford to shoot. Critics answer that generated diversity is representation without employment: the image shows a Black or plus-size model, but no Black or plus-size model is paid. Brands that want credibility tend to do both: hire diverse human talent for campaigns and use AI to extend coverage, and say so.

## Disclosure rules for AI models in fashion

### United States

Since June 9, 2026, New York has required businesses that produce ads to conspicuously disclose a synthetic performer when they know one appears in the ad, according to Cooley. The law targets AI people who are not recognizable as a real performer, which is exactly what stock and custom AI fashion models are. Penalties are $1,000 for a first violation and $5,000 for each later one. If an AI model endorses products as a virtual influencer, the FTC's Endorsement Guides also apply to the sponsored content.

### European Union

The EU AI Act's deepfake transparency obligations apply from August 2, 2026, according to Baker McKenzie. The draft "deepfake test" in the Commission's guidance covers realistic content involving real or fictitious persons that falsely appears authentic, so a photorealistic AI model in an ad can fall within scope. Platforms such as Meta and TikTok add their own AI labels on top.

The practical rule: label AI fashion models clearly and design the label into the layout. The Guess case shows that a hidden credit line does not prevent backlash.

## Which type of AI fashion model fits your brand

| Option | What it is | Rights and consent | Disclosure | Best for |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Stock AI model | A ready-made synthetic person from an AI fashion photography tool | Check the tool's license and exclusivity; no real person involved | Synthetic performer labels apply in ads (New York, EU) | Fast PDP images, small catalogs, testing |
| Custom AI model | A character designed for your brand, kept consistent with reference sheets | You own or license the character; no individual's likeness | Same as stock; build the label into the campaign | Recurring brand face, lookbooks, social |
| Digital twin of a real model | An AI replica of a real, consenting model | Written consent on scope, purpose, pay and duration (required in New York) | Depends on market; H&M chose watermarks | Brands that want a known face at scale |

## FAQ

### Are AI fashion models legal to use in ads?

Yes, but with conditions. A synthetic model that resembles no real person is generally legal, though New York has required a conspicuous disclosure in ads since June 2026, and the EU AI Act adds transparency duties. Replicating a real model requires their consent, which New York's Fashion Workers Act makes explicit and written.

### What is a digital twin in fashion?

A digital twin is an AI replica of a real model's face and body that a brand can use to create new images without a new shoot. H&M announced 30 of them in 2025, with the models keeping control of their replicas and being paid for their use. The contract should define channels, term, pay and approval rights.

### Do AI fashion models replace human models?

So far brands present them as a supplement, and Levi's, Mango and the agency behind the Guess ad all said so publicly. Critics such as the Model Alliance point out that each AI image can still remove paid work for models, photographers, stylists and hair and makeup artists. Most brands use a hybrid of real shoots and AI extensions.

### Why did the Levi's AI model announcement cause a backlash?

Levi's framed its 2023 test with Lalaland.ai as a way to increase diversity in its product images. Critics argued that generating diverse people instead of hiring them is representation without employment. Levi's then clarified that AI would supplement its shoots, not replace human models.

### Should I disclose that my model is AI-generated?

Yes. Disclosure is required in more and more places, including New York ads since June 2026 and the EU from August 2026, and platforms apply their own labels. Beyond the law, the Guess reaction in 2025 showed that audiences react worse to discovering AI than to being told up front.
